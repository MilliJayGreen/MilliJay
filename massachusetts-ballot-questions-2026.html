How I’m Voting on Massachusetts’ 2026 Ballot Questions, and Why

My choices on all nine questions, and the values behind them.

By MilliJay · October 21, 2026

Several people have asked me how I’m voting on the Massachusetts ballot questions, so here you go! 🗳️

One thing I strongly believe is that we seriously lack civic education in this country. Voting is more than just checking a box or choosing a political party. It’s about understanding what we’re voting for and how those decisions affect our communities.

I’m sharing my choices and the reasoning behind them. You don’t have to agree with me, and that’s perfectly fine! But I do encourage everyone to take the time to research these questions and make informed decisions before heading to the polls.

Question 1

Government Transparency

My vote: YES

Nobody should be above the law, especially our elected officials. In any workplace, employees are expected to be accountable for how they do their jobs. Why should our government be any different?

I believe in funding public services, but that comes with expecting transparency and accountability. We deserve to know how our government operates.

Question 2

Union Rights for Public Defender Employees

My vote: YES

I believe we need to bring unions back across this country! Public defenders, investigators, social workers, and other employees who serve our communities deserve a collective voice.

These are people doing incredibly important work, often under demanding conditions. They deserve the right to organize, negotiate fair wages, and advocate for better working conditions.

Question 3

Changing Primary Elections

My vote: HELL NO

I’m concerned that eliminating traditional party primaries and advancing only the top two candidates could give wealthy candidates even more influence.

Millionaires can already outspend ordinary people in elections. Imagine ending up with two wealthy candidates who don’t represent working-class communities.

From what I’ve observed, many people barely research candidates before general elections, much less primaries. Until we address that problem, I don’t support changing the system in a way that could further limit our choices.

Question 4

Same-Day Voter Registration

My vote: YES

Absolutely! Too many Americans lack access to proper civic education. Missing a registration deadline shouldn’t automatically mean an otherwise eligible person loses their opportunity to vote.

People move, circumstances change, and life happens. If someone is eligible and can provide the required documentation, they should have the opportunity to register on Election Day.

Question 5

Changing the State Tax Refund Formula

My vote: NO

Having lived in other states, I’ve come to appreciate Massachusetts and the investments we’ve made in healthcare, education, and public services.

We’ve seen how additional state revenue can support schools, transportation, and other important programs.

I would rather Massachusetts maintain the financial flexibility to invest in our communities and prepare for emergencies than change the formula in a way that could trigger more frequent tax refunds.

Especially with the uncertainty surrounding federal funding, I want our state to be financially prepared.

And this connects directly to Question 1. I’m comfortable with the government retaining revenue for public services, but I expect transparency and accountability in return.

Question 6

Nature for All Conservation Fund

My vote: NO

I absolutely support protecting our environment, drinking water, parks, and natural resources.

However, Massachusetts already has environmental programs, and I believe our elected lawmakers should maintain flexibility when deciding how to allocate public funds.

I trust our state government to balance environmental protection with healthcare, education, housing, and other community needs, provided there is transparency and accountability.

Question 7

Allowing Homes on Smaller Lots

My vote: YES

Massachusetts has become increasingly unaffordable, and gentrification has changed so many of our communities.

People my age who have worked hard their entire lives are struggling to afford their first home. That’s a serious problem.

Allowing more single-family homes on smaller lots could help increase housing opportunities. It won’t solve the entire housing crisis, but I believe we need to start making changes instead of continuing with the same restrictions.

Question 8

Ending Legal Recreational Marijuana Sales

My vote: HELL NO!

Making recreational marijuana sales illegal again isn’t going to make marijuana disappear.

People will continue purchasing and consuming it. My concern is that eliminating licensed recreational dispensaries could push more consumers toward unregulated street markets.

I’d rather have licensed businesses selling tested products than force consumers into markets without the same safety standards.

To me, this is about public health, consumer protection, and being realistic about how prohibition works.

Question 9

The 2024 Firearms Law

My vote: NO

I want strong gun safety, but I also believe in the right to self-defense.

Massachusetts already has strict laws, and I’m concerned that adding more restrictions burdens law-abiding residents while people with criminal intentions may still get guns illegally.

Given today’s political climate, protecting the ability to defend myself matters to me.

I understand that the 2024 law includes several different safety measures, but after considering the overall law, I’ve decided to vote against keeping it.

Why I’m sharing this

These decisions reflect my values, experiences, and what I believe our communities need.

I believe in workers’ rights, government accountability, accessible voting, affordable housing, strong public services, and individual freedoms. I also believe we should be willing to question policies, even when they come from politicians or parties we generally support.

You don’t have to vote the way I do. But please, understand what you’re voting for.

Democracy works better when we educate ourselves, ask questions, and hold our elected officials accountable.
